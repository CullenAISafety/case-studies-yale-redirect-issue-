# Case-Studies-Yale-Redirect-Issue-
Case Study -Yale University - Yale Entreprenurial Society Redirect Issue


1. Overview
This case study documents my independent observation and reporting of a redirect issue encountered while accessing the Yale Entrepreneurial Society (YES) website through Google Search.

The search result displayed the expected YES website, but following the link through Google's redirect confirmation flow led to an unrelated third-party domain, 1connectconsulting.com, rather than the expected destination.

I documented the behavior, repeated the observation over multiple days, and notified YES representatives. The issue was reported as resolved on October 6, 2026, according to my observations and records.

This case study documents the observed behavior and reporting process. It does not establish the root cause, the existence of a security breach, unauthorized access, or malicious intent.
2. Objectives
- Document unexpected website navigation and redirect behavior.
- Distinguish an observed symptom from a confirmed vulnerability.
- Record the notification and coordination process.
- Identify potential areas for further technical investigation.
- Demonstrate responsible reporting, evidence handling, and professional communication.
3. Scope and methodology
The investigation was limited to observing the publicly accessible search result and its navigation behavior.
1. Located the Yale Entrepreneurial Society website through Google Search.
2. Observed that the displayed destination appeared to correspond to YES.
3. Followed the link through Google's redirect confirmation flow.
4. Observed navigation to an unrelated third-party domain.
5. Repeated the observation over multiple days between October 1 and October 5, 2026.
6. Reported the issue to YES representatives and followed the communication process.
7. Recorded October 6, 2026, as the reported resolution date.
Limitations: This case study does not claim access to Google's internal systems, YES's hosting environment, DNS records, server logs, source code, or third-party infrastructure. No specific technical root cause has been confirmed in this report.

6. Potential technical explanations
The redirect could potentially involve a number of different systems, including:
- Search-result indexing or cached destination information.
- A URL redirect or intermediary link-handling service.
- Website configuration, routing, or third-party integrations.
- Browser extensions, local software, or network-level behavior.
- A change in the destination between the time a result was indexed and the time it was followed.
These are hypotheses for further investigation, not confirmed findings. Establishing the cause would require appropriate evidence, such as the exact URL, redirect chain, response headers, timestamps, and relevant system logs.


7. Responsible reporting and ethics
The purpose of this project is to document a potentially confusing navigation behavior and the reporting process, not to accuse a person or organization of wrongdoing.

The investigation should remain within publicly accessible, authorized testing boundaries. It should not involve bypassing access controls, probing unrelated infrastructure, collecting private information, or repeatedly testing systems without authorization.

Before publishing, redact personal email addresses, private correspondence, authentication tokens, and any information not necessary to demonstrate the finding. Obtain permission before publishing private communications or identifying individuals beyond their publicly documented roles.

8. Outcome and lessons learned
Based on my records, the unexpected redirect was reported as resolved on October 6, 2026. The exact technical fix and underlying cause remain unconfirmed unless supported by additional evidence from the responsible technical team.

Key lessons:
- A displayed search-result destination and its eventual navigation destination may differ.
- Reproducible observations and dated records improve the quality of a report.
- Clear escalation to organizational and technical contacts can support investigation.
- Resolution of a symptom does not independently prove the original cause.
- Security reporting should be factual, proportionate, and respectful of all parties.
